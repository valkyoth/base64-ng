# 2.0 Assurance And Protected Memory

Status: implemented in Commit 22 for the developing 2.0 API.

## Scope

The `secrets` feature exposes `base64_ng::assurance`. It combines two
independent capabilities before an assured operation can start:

1. An `AssuranceToken` borrowed from an `AssuranceContext` proves that the
   selected secret-algorithm, wipe/barrier, and, for attested policy,
   speculation generations remain current.
2. A `ProtectedSecret` proves ownership of one exact provider allocation and
   carries that allocation through `Uninitialized`, `Unvalidated`, and
   `Validated` typestates.

A token alone never authorizes an ordinary `&mut [u8]`. An allocation cannot
be reused for another assured operation after its unique owner is consumed.
The ordinary encode/decode APIs remain separate public-data surfaces.

## Assurance Levels

`BestEffort` is dependency-free and records the crate's complete logical-range
volatile overwrite plus selected barrier. It does not attest locked pages,
dump exclusion, hardware erasure, or speculative-execution behavior.

`Attested` additionally requires:

- an explicit `base64_ng_require_high_assurance` build;
- a reviewed `PlatformAttestation` implementation;
- evidence bound to the current target, provider identity, and provider
  generation;
- an attestable wipe posture and speculation barrier; and
- `ProtectionAttested` for the exact provider allocation.

The unsafe attestation boundary is a claim supplied by the deployment. The
type system prevents safe forgery, but it cannot independently measure the
hardware. A custom cfg, feature, target name, or requested policy is not by
itself attestation.

`secrets + simd` is additive. Ordinary public-data calls may select an admitted
SIMD backend, but assured secret operations remain in the scalar secret
implementation and revalidate only the generations relevant to that operation.
Ordinary backend quarantine therefore does not silently upgrade or downgrade a
secret token.

## Generation Model

`AssuranceContext` maintains separate monotonic generations for:

- ordinary backend health;
- secret scalar-algorithm policy;
- wipe primitive and barrier posture; and
- speculation posture.

Tokens are neither `Copy` nor `Clone`, borrow their context, and revalidate
before each assured transition. Allocation-specific protection is deliberately
not context-wide. Provider identity, provider generation, health generation,
and protection generation are captured by each protected owner and checked
independently.

## Typestate And Auto Traits

Only `Validated` storage can create an `ExposedProtectedSecret` view. The view
is explicit and redacted in `Debug`; its bytes remain owned and cleaned by the
protected allocation.

Tokens and protected owners are `!Sync`, `!UnwindSafe`, and
`!RefUnwindSafe`. These are API-friction signals, not a security boundary:
callers can use `AssertUnwindSafe`, and cleanup remains correct when they do.
Provider allocation handles and protected owners follow the same conservative
negative auto-trait posture. Protected owners are `Send` only through the crate-sealed
`ThreadMovableProvider` proof. External unsafe providers cannot self-admit that
cross-thread capability.

## Ordered Teardown

`try_close` and `Drop` use the same consuming sequence:

1. overwrite the complete logical allocation and execute the selected barrier;
2. obtain the wipe evidence required by the active assurance level;
3. remove physical protection;
4. reconcile accounting; and
5. dispose of the allocation conclusively.

No later hook runs after a failed, partial, stale, or indeterminate wipe. The
still-live handle moves directly into its pre-reserved quarantine slot.
Failures after a valid wipe preserve that wipe evidence while recording the
earliest incomplete stage.

Wipe, physical protection, accounting, and lifecycle are independent axes:

| Axis | Conservative states |
| --- | --- |
| Wipe | `WipeNotCompleted`, `WipedBestEffort`, `WipedAttested` |
| Physical protection | `ProtectionAttested`, `ProtectionConfirmedAbsent`, `ProtectionUnknown` |
| Accounting | `Charged`, `Reconciled` |
| Lifecycle | `Live`, `Closing`, `Quarantined`, `PermanentlyQuarantined`, `Tombstoned`, `Closed` |

`Charged + ProtectionUnknown` denies reuse; it does not claim that pages remain
locked. Indeterminate disposal is never retryable: the provider destroys every
addressable capability, retains only a non-owning tombstone identity, and
enters `Shutdown`.

Cleanup reports and errors contain only redacted stage, evidence, accounting,
retry, quarantine, and health categories. They never include bytes, addresses,
raw OS handles, registry identities, or page layouts.

## Bounded Default Provider

With `alloc`, `BestEffortProvider<SLOTS>` supplies a finite volatile provider
for dependency-free use. Construction sets limits for combined identities,
logical bytes, effective pages, registry entries, retry attempts, maintenance
work, and page size. Reservation happens before allocation and before plaintext
materialization. Every live identity carries a pre-reserved quarantine slot and
volatile monotonic teardown cursor, so quarantine transfer does not allocate.

`maintain` performs bounded work. Wipe retry restarts from byte zero. Exhausted
retry limits produce permanent quarantine and provider shutdown; the allocation
remains charged and unavailable for reuse until provider/process teardown.

The default provider does not lock memory, exclude dumps, attest hardware,
share pages, or persist recovery state. It is intentionally `!Sync` and admits
no cross-thread movement proof.

## Unsafe Provider Contract

`ProtectedMemoryProvider`, `PlatformAttestation`, and
`AttestationEvidence::new` are unsafe because false implementations can violate
ownership or assurance claims while exposing a safe API. Provider hooks must be
non-unwinding. Live handles must be unique and generation-safe. Byte slices must
cover exactly the complete logical allocation. Quarantine transfer must be
infallible and allocation-free. Reported operation dispositions must be
truthful, and ambiguous non-idempotent effects must not be replayed.
Every ownership-sensitive provider hook also requires an unconstructible
crate-issued `ProviderAccess`, preventing safe callers from obtaining a raw
default-provider handle outside `ProtectedSecret`.

Base 2.0 ships no persistent teardown provider. The unsafe extension protocol
alone does not constitute a persistent-recovery claim or stable persistent-
provider API. Any future persistent provider requires a separately reviewed
protocol for durability barriers, torn/corrupt records, rollback/replay,
generation uniqueness, authentication, redaction, bounded storage, and crash
recovery before it can make such a claim.

Commit 23 adds stable operation, exact-allocation, provider, and teardown
snapshots without changing this trust boundary. See
[`2.0_OPERATION_REPORTING.md`](2.0_OPERATION_REPORTING.md).

## Non-Claims

Successful cleanup proves only the selected overwrite/barrier procedure over
the provider's complete logical range. It does not prove eradication of
registers, caches, write buffers, compiler copies, stack history, allocator
history, swap, hibernation, snapshots, crash dumps, or storage outside that
range. Abort, process termination, `mem::forget`, and unsafe-provider contract
violations remain outside Rust `Drop` guarantees.

## Evidence

`scripts/check-2.0-assurance.sh` runs the feature/build matrix, normal and
attested operation tests, compile-fail token/typestate/auto-trait checks, and
the exact unsafe-boundary gate. `tests/v2_assurance.rs` fault-injects wipe,
protection, accounting, disposal, and indeterminate outcomes and asserts exact
teardown call order. Unit tests cover bounded reservation, generation
invalidation, redaction, retry exhaustion, and cleanup during
`AssertUnwindSafe` panic tests.
