# 2.0 Bounded Secret Decoding

Status: Implemented in Commit 19 for the emerging 2.0 API.

Commit 19 adds constant-time-oriented secret decoding under the dependency-free
`secrets` capability. This is a separate scalar state machine. It does not call
the ordinary `DecoderState`, accelerated decode backends, or secret-indexed
lookup tables. The limitations and evidence policy in `CONSTANT_TIME.md` still
apply: this is fixed-work, compiler-evidence-backed engineering, not a formally
verified hardware constant-time claim.

## Frame Types

- `SecretArrayFrame<N>` owns disjoint stack staging and final arrays. `N` is
  the maximum decoded length and every constructor enforces `N <= 1024`.
- `SecretFrame<'a>` binds caller-provided private staging and final output for
  the complete operation. Both ranges must hold the declared maximum decoded
  length.
- `SecretVecFrame`, with `alloc`, reserves and initializes separate staging and
  final allocations before accepting input. No secret frame reallocates after
  its first `update` call.
- `SecretDecoderState` exposes only public length, capacity, and failure
  metadata. Frames own its construction so a state cannot be detached from its
  storage contract.

`N` and runtime capacities always mean decoded bytes. The maximum accepted
encoded length is calculated separately with checked padded or unpadded Base64
length arithmetic. Padded text of one public length can represent different
decoded lengths; an otherwise valid value that exceeds decoded capacity is
therefore rejected opaquely at the result gate rather than through an early
content oracle.

## Fixed-Work Boundary

Every accepted encoded byte performs one complete 64-entry alphabet scan.
`update` progress depends only on the public chunk length and configured frame
capacity. Symbol validity, `=` position, canonical trailing bits, and the first
malformed position neither shorten the scan nor change update progress.

At most one four-symbol candidate quantum remains in state. Complete
non-final candidate plaintext is written only to private staging. Padding,
alphabet, canonicality, and decoded-capacity masks accumulate until `finish`.
Malformed `=` and malformed alphabet bytes receive the same scan shape.

The result gate ends the fixed-work validity boundary. On failure, no plaintext
is copied into final output and all frame-owned or decoder-managed staging is
wiped immediately, including pending bytes, decoded values, validity masks,
and accumulated invalidity. On success, validity and exact decoded length
become public; a length-proportional copy into final secret storage occurs
after the gate. Whole-call timing therefore includes a deliberate success-only
copy and is not claimed to hide success from failure.

`InputTooLarge` is decided from public lengths before symbol scanning and moves
the state into an absorbing failed phase. Owned frames preflight complete
capacity and cannot report `OutputFull` after updates begin. Borrowed frame
capacity errors are likewise decided before content processing.

## Storage Separation And Cleanup

Private staging, encoded input, and final output must be byte-disjoint. The
borrowed constructor checks staging against final output, and every update
checks that chunk against both complete ranges before content-dependent work.
Checked address arithmetic rejects overflow. An unsafe external storage or
protection provider cannot waive byte disjointness; it must establish valid,
non-aliased Rust references before calling the safe API.

Stack and heap frames own both staging and final storage. They wipe complete
capacities on failure, unwind, cancellation through drop, or ordinary drop.
Successful output retains fixed/preallocated capacity, keeps its unused tail
zero, exposes only the initialized prefix, and cannot grow through the secret
owner API.

For `SecretFrame`, the decoder wipes the complete staging range. A returned
`SecretOutput` owns final-range cleanup. The caller or protection provider
still owns allocation-level unlock and disposal. Cleanup does not run after
`mem::forget`, abort, process termination, or hostile control-flow that skips
destructors, and it cannot erase historical copies outside owned ranges.

## Example

```rust
use base64_ng::{
    STRICT_STANDARD_PADDED,
    secret::{SecretArrayFrame, SecretInput},
};

let mut frame = SecretArrayFrame::<32>::new(&STRICT_STANDARD_PADDED)?;
frame.update(&SecretInput::new(b"c2Vj"))?;
frame.update(&SecretInput::new(b"cmV0"))?;
let decoded = frame.finish()?;
assert_eq!(decoded.expose_secret().as_bytes(), b"secret");
# Ok::<(), base64_ng::secret::SecretDecodeError>(())
```

## Evidence

`scripts/check-2.0-secret-decoder.sh` runs:

- strict-profile and validated-custom-alphabet differential tests;
- malformed-class, malformed-position, canonicality, padding, and capacity
  tests with opaque errors;
- equal-public-length progress and 64-scan work-accounting tests;
- delayed-public-write, zero-tail, absorbing-failure, drop, and unwind cleanup
  tests;
- checked overlap and address-overflow tests;
- compile-fail cases for direct, macro, and generic construction above the
  1,024-byte stack limit; and
- `no_std`, all-feature, public API, and strict Clippy matrices.

`scripts/check_kani_advanced.sh` always code-generates the secret harnesses with
`secrets`. Setting `BASE64_NG_KANI_PROVE_SECRET_FRAMES=1` proves the bounded
release, early oversize rejection, and borrowed failure-cleanup harnesses.
Timing runs must compare equal public lengths across malformed positions and
classes, compare distinct valid contents, and distinguish the pre-gate core
from whole-call success declassification.
