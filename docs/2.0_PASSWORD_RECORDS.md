# 2.0 Password-Record Base64 Transforms

`base64-ng-password` owns exact field and record transforms for five formats:

| Public algorithm | Identifier | Checksum input |
|---|---|---|
| `PasslibPbkdf2Algorithm::Sha1` | `$pbkdf2$` | 20 already-derived bytes |
| `PasslibPbkdf2Algorithm::Sha256` | `$pbkdf2-sha256$` | 32 already-derived bytes |
| `PasslibPbkdf2Algorithm::Sha512` | `$pbkdf2-sha512$` | 64 already-derived bytes |
| `ShaCryptAlgorithm::Sha256` | `$5$` | 32-byte final SHA-crypt digest |
| `ShaCryptAlgorithm::Sha512` | `$6$` | 64-byte final SHA-crypt digest |

The Passlib records follow the 1.7.4
[PBKDF2 format documentation](https://passlib.readthedocs.io/en/stable/lib/passlib.hash.pbkdf2_digest.html).
Their salt and checksum fields use standard Base64 bit grouping, replace `+`
with `.`, omit padding, require positive decimal rounds without leading zeroes,
and enforce checksum sizes of 27, 43, and 86 encoded characters.

The SHA-crypt records follow Ulrich Drepper's
[SHA-crypt specification](https://www.akkadia.org/drepper/SHA-crypt.txt).
They admit only `$5$` and `$6$`, the `./0-9A-Za-z` salt alphabet, salts of at
most 16 characters, the implicit 5,000-round form or explicit canonical
`rounds=N$` in the inclusive range 1,000 through 999,999,999, and the exact
algorithm-specific digest-byte permutations. The generic core crypt alphabet
codec is not used as a substitute for those permutations.

## Security Boundary

The companion does not accept passwords, derive PBKDF2 or SHA digests, hash
passwords, compare records, verify passwords, choose salts, or recommend a
password-storage scheme. Generation accepts already-derived bytes. Parsing
returns borrowed encoded fields, which require explicitly named exposure or
decode operations.

Records may contain password-verifier material. Their custom field-selective
redacted `Debug` shows only algorithm, rounds, lengths, and `[REDACTED]` field
markers. There is no record `Display` implementation. Errors contain stable
classes and optional positions/capacities but never malformed raw fragments.
This ordinary parser is not a constant-time secret processor and does not
promise cleanup of caller-owned records or decoded output.

## Bounds And Atomicity

`PasswordRecordLimits` independently bounds record bytes, field bytes,
decoded salt bytes, decoded output, generated output, and pre-output work.
Caller-owned one-shot destinations are transactional: all structure,
canonicality, exact-length, limit, and capacity checks complete before output
is changed. Allocation helpers reserve only the validated logical record size.
